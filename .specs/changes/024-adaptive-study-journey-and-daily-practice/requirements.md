# Requirements

These requirements were redistributed without deletion: contract/adapter to C28, ledger/profile to C29, adaptive modes/rewards to C30, public explanations to C31, and academic evaluation to C32.

## Study Journey

- Replace independent client-side counters with one account-scoped profile endpoint that returns progress, due reviews, simulator activity, recent assessment evidence, rewards, missions, and a ranked next action.
- Explain every recommendation with its trigger, reviewed source, and intended learning outcome.
- Present learning evidence separately from activity, points, motivation prompts, and implementation status.

## Learning modes

- Daily Challenge: a short spaced-retrieval session drawn from due and weak reviewed content.
- Chapter assessment: a broader diagnostic/mastery checkpoint offered manually and when a reviewed chapter is substantially complete.
- Guided remediation: feedback, graduated hints, a source link, and a near-transfer retry after an error or low-confidence correct answer.
- Simulator recommendation: offered only when the simulator maps to the learner's current reviewed section and the action has a stated learning purpose.
- Simulator path: request a prediction, record a meaningful manipulation, ask for comparison/reflection, and schedule a later retrieval when the simulator supports it.

## Adaptive selection

- Prioritize overdue weak concepts, prerequisite gaps, recent low-confidence answers, and items needing successful retrieval in a later session.
- Keep a smaller interleaved share of stable or recently correct material to prevent narrow drilling.
- Use errors to shorten the next interval; use repeated unaided success across sessions to lengthen it.
- Do not mark mastery after one correct answer.
- Never recommend content from locked Chapters 8–13.
- Use a versioned reviewed concept/prerequisite graph and treat one response as noisy evidence rather than permanent mastery or failure.

## Gamification mechanics

- Ship the content-linked retrieval/feedback loop before adding optional structural mechanics.
- Define and version a mechanism card for every point, mission, badge, streak, timer, or social element.
- Keep public individual leaderboards disabled. Any future exception requires a separate Change with opt-in, pseudonyms, bounded cohorts, opt-out, and discouragement monitoring.
- Never allow points to alter answers, grades, assessment difficulty, exemptions, or mastery thresholds.
- Add implementation-fidelity events for eligibility, exposure, action, and opt-out.

## AI boundary

- AI may rank eligible reviewed items or generate a bounded variant only from approved source passages and exercise manifests.
- Every generated exercise must retain source provenance, validation state, difficulty, skill tags, and a report-error route.
- The system must work with deterministic recommendations when AI is unavailable.

## Risks

- An opaque recommender can undermine learner trust.
- Sparse evidence can produce false precision; show uncertainty and offer learner choice.
- Incorrect concept tagging can amplify a bad recommendation.
- Optimizing time, attempts, or points can improve activity metrics while leaving physics learning unchanged.
- Social comparison can discourage lower-ranked learners even when average satisfaction increases.

## Academic evaluation

- Define separate learning, behavior, experience, and equity/safety outcomes before rollout.
- Capture baseline knowledge, assignment/exposure, attrition, and configuration version.
- Require changed-form or delayed evidence before claiming retention or mastery.
- Report effect sizes and uncertainty; do not translate motivation or activity into achievement claims.
