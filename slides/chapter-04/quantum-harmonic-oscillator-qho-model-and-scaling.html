QHO: differential equation, variables and eigenstates
From parabolic confinement to the Hermite equation and the allowed stationary states
2 / 14
Guided reading

The QHO is described by a parabolic potential. The book introduces the dimensionless coordinate \(\xi=\alpha x\), obtains the Hermite differential equation, identifies its solutions, and then writes the normalized stationary eigenfunctions.

From Hooke's law to a Hamiltonian

For \(F=-kx\), the potential is \(V(x)=kx^2/2\). Writing \(\omega=\sqrt{k/m}\) gives

\[\hat H=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2}+\frac12m\omega^2x^2\]

The potential rises without bound on both sides, so the stationary states are bound.

Dimensionless variables

The variables used in the book are

\[\xi=\alpha x,\qquad \alpha=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}},\qquad \epsilon=\frac{E}{\hbar\omega}\]

With these definitions, the stationary Schrödinger equation becomes dimensionless.

Hermite differential equation

The dimensionless equation is

\[\frac{d^2\psi}{d\xi^2}+(2\epsilon-\xi^2)\psi=0\]

After extracting the Gaussian factor, \(\psi(\xi)=\chi(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}\), the remaining function satisfies

\[\frac{d^2\chi}{d\xi^2}-2\xi\frac{d\chi}{d\xi}+(2\epsilon-1)\chi=0\]
Solutions and stationary eigenfunctions

The solutions of this Hermite equation are \(\chi_n(\xi)=A_nH_n(\xi)\). Restoring the Gaussian and normalizing gives

\[\psi_n(x)=\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\frac{e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}}{\sqrt{2^n n!}}H_n\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}x\right)\]
