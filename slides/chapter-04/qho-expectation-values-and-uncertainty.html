QHO: expectation values and uncertainty
Position and momentum moments in Appendix 4.A
5 / 14
Guided reading

Appendix 4.A derives expectation values for position and momentum in the quantum harmonic oscillator. It first uses the parity of the Hermite-polynomial eigenfunctions and then obtains the momentum moments from the Ehrenfest and virial theorems.

Position moments and parity

The book begins with the definition

\[\langle\hat x^q\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_n^*(x)\hat x^q\psi_n(x)\,dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^q\psi_n^2(x)\,dx.\]

Because the QHO wave functions are real and \(\psi_n^2(x)\) is even, all odd moments vanish:

\[\langle\hat x^q\rangle=0\qquad\text{for odd }q.\]
Dimensionless recurrence

With \(\xi=\alpha x\), \(\alpha=\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\), and \(\psi_n(\xi)=\sqrt\alpha\,f(\xi)\), Eq. (4.A.7) relates the even dimensionless moments:

\[\frac q2(q-1)\langle\xi^{q-2}\rangle+2(2n+1)\langle\xi^q\rangle-2\frac{q+2}{q+1}\langle\xi^{q+2}\rangle=0.\]

Starting from \(\langle\xi^0\rangle=1\), it gives

\[\langle\hat x^2\rangle=\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\left(n+\frac12\right).\]
Momentum moments

For momentum, Appendix 4.A restricts the calculation to \(\langle\hat p\rangle\) and \(\langle\hat p^2\rangle\). From the Ehrenfest theorem, since \(\langle\hat x\rangle=0\),

\[\langle\hat p\rangle=0.\]

The virial theorem gives

\[\langle\hat p^2\rangle=m^2\omega^2\langle\hat x^2\rangle=m\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right).\]
Variance and uncertainty

Using the moments above, the appendix writes

\[\operatorname{var}(x)=\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\left(n+\frac12\right),\qquad \operatorname{var}(p)=m\hbar\omega\left(n+\frac12\right).\]

Therefore the product of standard deviations is

\[\Delta x\,\Delta p=\hbar\left(n+\frac12\right).\]
What the recurrence supplies

The recurrence in Eq. (4.A.7) is for even values of \(q\). The first step, \(q=0\), supplies \(\langle\hat x^2\rangle\); setting \(q=2\) supplies the next even moment. The book therefore obtains the position moments in sequence rather than introducing a different oscillator notation.

The stated comparison

The result \(\Delta x\,\Delta p=\hbar(n+1/2)\) is to be interpreted in terms of the Heisenberg uncertainty principle. In particular, the ground state \(n=0\) gives the value \(\hbar/2\).

Link to the oscillator eigenfunctions

The parity statement used here comes from the Hermite-polynomial eigenfunctions discussed in Section 4.1.2; the expectation-value calculation then uses those stationary states without changing their notation.
