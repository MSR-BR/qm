Probability current, reflection and transmission
Flux, not amplitude alone, measures one-dimensional scattering
9 / 14
Guided reading

Scattering states are not square-normalizable over all space. Their physical normalization is instead expressed through probability current: the incident flux splits into reflected and transmitted flux.

Probability current
\[j=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\psi^*\frac{d\psi}{dx}-\psi\frac{d\psi^*}{dx}\right)=\frac{\hbar}{m}\operatorname{Im}\!\left(\psi^*\psi'\right)\]

For a plane wave \(Ae^{ikx}\), \(j=+(\hbar k/m)|A|^2\); for \(Be^{-ikx}\), \(j=-(\hbar k/m)|B|^2\).

Continuity equation
\[\frac{\partial |\Psi|^2}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial j}{\partial x}=0\]

In a stationary, source-free one-dimensional problem, total current is constant across the interaction region.

Scattering ansatz
\[\psi_I=Ae^{ik_1x}+Be^{-ik_1x},\qquad \psi_{III}=Fe^{ik_3x}\]

No left-moving term appears on the far right when particles are incident only from the left.

Reflection and transmission
\[R=\frac{|j_{\rm ref}|}{j_{\rm inc}}=\frac{|B|^2}{|A|^2}\]
\[T=\frac{j_{\rm trans}}{j_{\rm inc}}=\frac{k_3}{k_1}\frac{|F|^2}{|A|^2}\]

For real, time-independent potentials, current conservation gives \(R+T=1\).

Deriving current for a plane wave

Insert \(Ae^{ikx}\) into \(j=(\hbar/m)\operatorname{Im}(\psi^*\psi')\). Since \(\psi'=ik\psi\), the result is \(j=\hbar k|A|^2/m\). Reversing the exponent reverses the sign. For a superposition, cross terms cancel in the stationary net current, leaving incident flux minus reflected flux. The sign therefore carries direction, while \(|A|^2\) alone does not.

Conservation at the interface

For a real potential, Schrödinger evolution preserves probability. In a stationary scattering state, \(\partial|\Psi|^2/\partial t=0\), so the continuity equation requires \(dj/dx=0\). Evaluating far from the interaction region gives \(j_{in}+j_{ref}=j_{trans}\). Dividing by positive incident current produces \(R+T=1\). Failure of this check usually signals a missing wave-number factor or an incorrect matching sign.

Why the velocity factor matters

Probability density alone counts amplitude, but flux also counts speed. When the asymptotic potentials differ, \(k_1\neq k_3\), so \(T\) is not simply \(|F/A|^2\).
