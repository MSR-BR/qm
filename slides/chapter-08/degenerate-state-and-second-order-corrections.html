Degenerate case: second-order energy

COUPLING TO STATES OUTSIDE THE DEGENERACY DOMAIN
7 / 10

Guided reading

After the first-order reduced problem has chosen \(\lvert n_b^{(0)}\rangle\), the second-order energy comes from its coupling to states outside the reference domain.

Projected second-order equation

Using the energy expansion through second order and the vector expansion through first order gives:

\[E_{n_b}^{(2)}\lvert n_b^{(0)}\rangle+E_{n_b}^{(1)}\lvert n_b^{(1)}\rangle=\hat B\left(\hat W\lvert n_a^{(1)}\rangle+\hat W\lvert n_b^{(1)}\rangle\right).\]

Example 8.8

For the chapter's three-dimensional degenerate example, the corrections are:

\[E_{1a}^{(2)}=-5,\qquad E_{1b}^{(2)}=\frac12,\qquad E_{2b}^{(2)}=\frac92,\] \[E_{1a}=\lambda-5\lambda^2,\qquad E_{1b}=1-2\lambda+\frac{\lambda^2}{2},\qquad E_{2b}=1+2\lambda+\frac{9\lambda^2}{2}.\]

Energy correction

The contribution involving \(\lvert n_b^{(1)}\rangle\) vanishes because \(\hat W_B\) is diagonal in the selected basis and the correction excludes the reference vector. The final result is:

\[E_{n_b}^{(2)}=\sum_{k\notin B}\frac{\left\lvert\langle k^{(0)}\vert\hat W\vert n_b^{(0)}\rangle\right\rvert^2}{E_b^{(0)}-E_k^{(0)}}.\]

Boundary of the sum

Unlike the non-degenerate expression, the sum explicitly excludes all states in \(B\). The internal degeneracy was already resolved by diagonalizing \(\hat W_B\) at first order.
