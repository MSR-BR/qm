Levi-Civita symbol

Appendix 6.A defines \(\epsilon_{ijk}\) as a rank-3 tensor with values \(+1\), \(-1\), or \(0\), depending on the permutation of the indices.

\[\epsilon_{ijk}=\begin{cases}+1, & \text{even permutations},\\ -1, & \text{odd permutations},\\ 0, & i=j\ \text{or}\ i=k\ \text{or}\ j=k.\end{cases}\]
Einstein notation

The book uses the convention that a repeated index indicates summation over that index.

\[\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{imn}\equiv \sum_i \epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{imn}\]
Kronecker-delta identity

The reduced identity used in the commutator manipulation is

\[\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{imn}=\delta_{jm}\delta_{kn}-\delta_{jn}\delta_{km}\]
Use in Chapter 6

The same notation writes the angular-momentum operator as \(\hat L_i=\epsilon_{ijk}\hat x_j\hat p_k\). The detailed commutator proof is in Box 6.1, not in the HTML flow.

Physical reading

The notation is not an additional physical assumption; it is a compact way to encode the vector product and the noncommuting component structure that was stated in item 6.2.
