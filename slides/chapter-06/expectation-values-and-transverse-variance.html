Consequences: transverse components
Expectation values, variances and the cone picture
9 / 15
Guided reading

Section 6.3 uses the ladder operators to evaluate what is known about \(L_x\) and (L_y) in the state (|l,m angle).

Transverse expectation values

Using \(\hat L_x\) and \(\hat L_y\) in terms of \(\hat L_\pm\), the book obtains

\[\langle\hat L_x\rangle=\langle\hat L_y\rangle=0\]

The reason is that \(\langle l,m|l,m\pm1\rangle=0\).

Quadratic expectation values
\[\langle\hat L_x^2\rangle=\frac{\hbar^2}{2}\left[l(l+1)-m^2\right]\]

The same result holds for \(\langle\hat L_y^2\rangle\).

Standard deviations
\[\Delta L_x=\Delta L_y=\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt2}\left[l(l+1)-m^2\right]^{1/2}\]
\[\Delta L_z=0\]

The book concludes that after \(L_z\) is measured precisely, \(L_x\) and \(L_y\) cannot also be determined precisely.

Cone interpretation

The text describes angular momentum as residing on the surface area of a cone with central axis along \(z\) and height \(m\hbar\). For maximum projection, \(m=l\),

\[\Delta L_x(m=l)=\hbar\sqrt{\frac{l}{2}}\]

Even at maximum projection, the angular momentum is not absolutely aligned along the \(z\) axis.
