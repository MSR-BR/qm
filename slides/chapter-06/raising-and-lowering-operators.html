Raising and lowering operators
Definitions, commutators, products and Hermitian conjugates
5 / 15
Guided reading

Definition 6.2 introduces the raising and lowering operators. The book then derives their commutators, products and Hermitian conjugates.

Definition 6.2
\[\hat L_\pm=\hat L_x\pm i\hat L_y\]

The Cartesian components can be recovered as

\[\hat L_x=\frac12(\hat L_++\hat L_-),\qquad \hat L_y=\frac{1}{2i}(\hat L_+-\hat L_-)\]
Commutators
\[[\hat L_\pm,\hat L_\mp]=\pm2\hbar\hat L_z\]
\[[\hat L_z,\hat L_\pm]=\pm\hbar\hat L_\pm\]
\[[\hat L^2,\hat L_\pm]=0\]

The last relation means that ladder operations preserve the eigenvalue of \(\hat L^2\).

Products
\[\hat L_\pm\hat L_\mp=\hat L^2-\hat L_z^2\pm\hbar\hat L_z\]

The product formulas are used later to obtain endpoint conditions and normalization factors.

Non-Hermitian ladder operators

The angular-momentum components are Hermitian observables, but the ladder operators are not Hermitian:

\[\hat L_\pm^\dagger=\hat L_\mp\]
