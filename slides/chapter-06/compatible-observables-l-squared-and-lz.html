Compatible observables: L squared and Lz
The common eigenbasis begins with a commuting pair
4 / 15
Guided reading

Section 6.2 begins by introducing \(L^2\). Since the components do not commute with each other, the book seeks another operator that commutes with them.

Definition of total angular momentum
\[\hat L^2=\hat L_x^2+\hat L_y^2+\hat L_z^2\]

Theorem 6.1 states that this operator commutes with each component \(\hat L_u\).

Theorem 6.1
\[[\hat L^2,\hat L_u]=0\]

The proof is shown in the book for \(u=z\), and the same idea applies to the other directions.

Choice of reference axis

Since \(L^2\) commutes with all components, the magnitude and one projection can be known together. The book chooses \(\hat L_z\) by convention.

Definition 6.1

The simultaneous eigenvectors are first written as \(|a,b\rangle\):

\[\hat L^2|a,b\rangle=a|a,b\rangle,\qquad \hat L_z|a,b\rangle=b|a,b\rangle\]

The goal is then to determine the values of \(a\) and \(b\).
