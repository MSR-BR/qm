Change of basis for Lx
The l = 1 eigenvectors of Lx and diagonal form
11 / 15
Guided reading

Example 6.2 is similar to the Stern-Gerlach discussion in Chapter 3: \(L_x\) is not diagonal in the (|l,m angle) basis, but it is diagonal in its own eigenbasis.

Eigenvalue equation
\[\hat L_x|l,m\rangle_x=\lambda |l,m\rangle_x\]

For \(l=1\), the book obtains three eigenvalues:

\[\lambda=+\hbar,\qquad \lambda=0,\qquad \lambda=-\hbar\]
Eigenvectors of Lx
\[|1,+1\rangle_x=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1\\\sqrt2\\1\end{pmatrix},\qquad |1,0\rangle_x=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt2\\0\\-\sqrt2\end{pmatrix},\qquad |1,-1\rangle_x=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1\\-\sqrt2\\1\end{pmatrix}\]
Basis transformation

The book summarizes the relation as

\[|m\rangle_x=\hat U|m\rangle\]

and also writes the inverse relation \(|m\rangle=\hat U^{-1}|m\rangle_x\).

Diagonal result
\[(\hat L_x)_x=\hat U(\hat L_x)_z\hat U^\dagger\]
\[(\hat L_x)_x=\hbar\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\]

The text interprets this operation as a rotation of the axes, where the \(x\)-axis takes the role previously associated with the \(z\)-axis.
