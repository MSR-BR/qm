Chapter 6 · Item 6.13
Spherical harmonics as eigenfunctions
Recovering the Chapter 5 angular functions from L squared and Lz
13 / 15
Guided reading

Section 6.5 returns to the angular equation from Chapter 5 and shows that spherical harmonics are eigenfunctions of \(L^2\) and \(L_z\).

Angular equation from Chapter 5
\[\left[\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)+\frac{1}{\sin^2\theta}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial\phi^2}\right]Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)=-l(l+1)Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\]

The book uses \(Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)=A_l^m\Theta(\theta)\Phi(\phi)\) and \(\lambda=l(l+1)\).

Eigenvalue equations
\[\hat L^2Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)=l(l+1)\hbar^2Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\]
\[\hat L_zY_l^m(\theta,\phi)=m\hbar Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\]

These equations recover, in position space, the eigenvalue equations for angular momentum.

Example 6.3

For

\[Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)=\frac14\sqrt{\frac{15}{2\pi}}\sin^2(\theta)e^{2i\phi}\]

the book applies \(\hat L_z\) and \(\hat L^2\), obtaining \(m=+2\) and \(l=2\).

Examples 6.4 and 6.5

Using the lowering operator equation \(\hat L_-Y_1^{-1}=0\), Example 6.4 obtains

\[Y_1^{-1}(\theta,\phi)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{8\pi}}\sin\theta e^{-i\phi}\]

Example 6.5 converts \(A(x,y,z)=\alpha z/\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}\) to \(A(\theta,\phi)=\alpha\cos\theta\), normalizes it, and obtains \(A(\theta,\phi)=Y_1^0(\theta,\phi)\). Thus \(P(1,\pm1)=0\) and \(P(1,0)=1\).
