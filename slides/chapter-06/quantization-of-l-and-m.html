Ladder action and endpoints
Finite ladders force upper and lower states
6 / 15
Guided reading

Corollaries 6.3 to 6.5 show how \(L_\pm\) changes the eigenvalue \(b\), why endpoints must exist and why the lower endpoint is the negative of the upper one.

Corollary 6.3
\[\hat L_\pm|a,b\rangle=c_\pm|a,b\pm\hbar\rangle\]

The vector after the ladder operation has the same eigenvalue \(a\) of \(\hat L^2\), but the eigenvalue of \(\hat L_z\) changes by \(\pm\hbar\).

Positivity bound
\[\langle a,b|\hat L^2-\hat L_z^2|a,b\rangle=a-b^2\ge0\]

Therefore \(a\ge b^2\). For fixed \(a\), the possible values of \(b\) cannot extend without bound.

Endpoint states
\[\hat L_+|a,b_{\max}\rangle=0,\qquad \hat L_-|a,b_{\min}\rangle=0\]

Using the operator products, the endpoint conditions give

\[a=b_{\max}(b_{\max}+\hbar),\qquad a=b_{\min}(b_{\min}-\hbar)\]
Symmetric range

Comparing the endpoint equations yields

\[b_{\min}+b_{\max}=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad b_{\min}=-b_{\max}\]

Thus \(b\) is confined between \(-b_{\max}\) and \(+b_{\max}\).
